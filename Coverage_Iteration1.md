# LF coverage — iteration 1

Baseline measurement of the LFs shipped in `commit_labels/lfs.py`, before any tuning. Generated by `gen_coverage_md.py`; re-applies every LF to `data/interim/commits.parquet` rather than reading the stored columns in `commits_labeled.parquet`, so `lfs.py` is the only source of truth.

- **72,166 commits**, 12 repos, **20 LFs** (10 positive / 10 negative)
- Gold: **49** advisory-confirmed fixes (0.068% of the corpus)
- Of those, **10 change no code** (version/changelog only — see `docs/cve-fix-commits.md`), so the code-bearing gold set is **39**. Both numbers are reported throughout.
- `PandasLFApplier` over the full corpus: 23.6s
- LabelModel: `--class-balance 0.03`, `--threshold 0.5`, 500 epochs, seed 42

## Why recall is 2 and not 9

Majority vote recovers **9** gold fixes; the fitted LabelModel recovers **2**. The LFs are not what lost the other 7 — the model is. Every gold fix that any positive LF votes on:

| Commit | Positive LFs firing | `prob_security` | Majority vote | LabelModel @ 0.5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [`701fea6848`](docs/cve-fix-commits.md#701fea6848da) | `lf_fix_plus_security_noun` | 0.2720 | security | not-sec |
| [`00fddf4442`](docs/cve-fix-commits.md#00fddf444220) | `lf_cve_id`, `lf_ghsa_id` | 0.7997 | security | **security** |
| [`77ad41678b`](docs/cve-fix-commits.md#77ad41678b45) | `lf_fix_plus_security_noun`, `lf_security_paths_and_fix` | 0.8190 | security | **security** |
| [`ccedd064ca`](docs/cve-fix-commits.md#ccedd064ca56) | `lf_vuln_class` | 0.2718 | security | not-sec |
| [`28080d80fc`](docs/cve-fix-commits.md#28080d80fc8a) | `lf_vuln_class` | 0.2718 | security | not-sec |
| [`9bb2878319`](docs/cve-fix-commits.md#9bb2878319d5) | `lf_fix_plus_security_noun` | 0.2720 | security | not-sec |
| [`58b46c81c6`](docs/cve-fix-commits.md#58b46c81c679) | `lf_vuln_class` | 0.2718 | security | not-sec |
| [`255bccc1ba`](docs/cve-fix-commits.md#255bccc1badd) | `lf_advisory_language` | 0.2684 | security | not-sec |
| [`162321e899`](docs/cve-fix-commits.md#162321e899cc) | `lf_ghsa_id` | 0.2644 | security | not-sec |

One positive vote buys `prob_security` ≈ 0.27, which loses to the `--threshold 0.5` cutoff every time. Only the two commits where *two* positive LFs co-fired clear it. With the shipped configuration **a single positive LF vote can never produce a positive label**, no matter which LF it is — so adding another narrow, rarely-co-firing positive LF will not move recall on its own.

Threshold sweep on the same fitted model:

| Threshold | Flagged `security` | % of corpus | Gold recovered |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.9 | 70 | 0.10% | 0 of 49 |
| 0.75 | 311 | 0.43% | 2 of 49 |
| 0.5 ←current | 311 | 0.43% | 2 of 49 |
| 0.3 | 320 | 0.44% | 2 of 49 |
| 0.27 | 1,855 | 2.57% | 7 of 49 |
| 0.2 | 2,044 | 2.83% | 9 of 49 |
| 0.1 | 2,045 | 2.83% | 9 of 49 |

The mass is nearly degenerate: every cutoff from 0.3 to 0.75 gives the exact same 311 commits, and the entire gain from 2 to 9 happens in the 0.20–0.27 band at a cost of ~1,700 extra flagged commits. So the real question for iteration 2 is not the threshold — it is why `class_balance = 0.03` plus 10 near-disjoint positive LFs makes the model conclude the positives are anti-correlated with the class. Worth testing: fewer, broader positive LFs; a learned class balance; or Snorkel's dependency structure for the three advisory-style LFs the README already flags as correlated.

*(The fit also emits `divide by zero`/`overflow` `RuntimeWarning`s from `label_model.py:419` — `log(mu)` on cells no LF ever populates. Expected with LFs this sparse, but it means the fit is running near the edge of its numerics.)*

## What to change first

Read off the numbers above, not from intuition:

1. **The model, not the vocabulary, is the current bottleneck.** 9 of 49 gold fixes already get a positive vote, and the LabelModel converts only 2 of them. Fix that before writing a single new regex.
